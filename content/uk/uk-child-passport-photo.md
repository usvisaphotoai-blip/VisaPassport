Getting a **UK child passport photo** right can feel tricky. Children wriggle, babies fall asleep at the wrong moment, and one small mistake can mean your application is sent back. The good news is that the rules for a **UK baby and child passport photo** are simple once you know them, and there is real flexibility for the youngest ones.

This guide explains the size, background and expression rules in plain English, split by age. It also gives practical tips for taking a photo at home, including for a newborn, and shows how a **baby passport photo maker online** can save you time and stress.

## UK Child Passport Photo Requirements at a Glance

Whether your child is three months or thirteen years old, the photo must meet the same core standards used for adult UK passports.

| Requirement    | Detail                                                                  |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Print size     | 35 mm wide x 45 mm tall, 2 identical photos for paper applications      |
| Head size      | 29-34 mm from chin to crown                                             |
| Digital format | JPEG, at least 600 x 750 pixels, 50 KB to 10 MB                         |
| Colour         | Colour photo only, no black and white, no filters or effects            |
| Background     | Plain, light-coloured (cream or light grey)                             |
| Face           | Fully visible, no shadows, no red-eye                                   |
| Quality        | Sharp, in focus, not blurry or pixelated, and not edited or "corrected" |
| Photo age      | Taken in the last month                                                 |

These are the same measurements used across most UK ID documents, so a correctly sized photo is also handy for other forms.

## UK Child Passport Photo Rules by Age

The rules relax for young children, but only up to a point. This table shows what changes at each age.

| Age        | Eyes open?          | Look at the camera? | Neutral expression? |
| ---------- | ------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| Under 1    | Not required        | Not required        | Not required        |
| 1 to 5     | Yes, where possible | Not required        | Not required        |
| 6 and over | Yes                 | Yes                 | Yes, mouth closed   |

For every age, the child must be alone in the picture, with the whole face visible, no toys or dummies, and a plain light background.

## Rules for a UK Baby Passport Photo

Photographing a baby is the part most parents worry about. The rules recognise that a newborn cannot follow instructions, so several requirements are relaxed for babies under one year old.

**What is allowed for babies under 1:**

- Your baby's eyes do not need to be open
- Your baby does not need to look at the camera
- A relaxed or sleepy expression is fine, as long as the whole face can be seen
- You can support your baby's head with your hand, as long as your hand is not visible in the photo

**What is still required:**

- Your baby's head must be fully visible and centred
- No other person, including a parent's hand or arm, can be in the photo
- No toys, dummies or comforters in the shot
- The background must be plain and light, with no patterns or creases
- The photo must be clear, evenly lit and taken from the front or directly above

### A Simple Trick for Newborns

Lay your baby on their back on a plain, light-coloured sheet or blanket, such as a white or cream cot sheet. Stand over them and take the picture from directly above. This gives you the plain background you need without anyone having to hold your baby up. Make sure the sheet is smooth so there are no folds casting shadows.

Never leave your baby alone on a bed or sofa while you check the photos. Put everything you need within reach first, and keep one hand near your baby at all times, just out of the frame.

### How to Take a Newborn Passport Photo Step by Step

1. **Pick a calm moment.** Choose a time when your baby is fed, rested and comfortable, with a clean nappy. Avoid the minutes just before a nap.
2. **Prepare the space.** Smooth a plain, light-coloured sheet over a firm, safe surface near a window. Lay your baby on their back, and fold a small rolled blanket under the sheet to keep the head steady if needed.
3. **Use soft daylight.** Place your baby so light from a window falls evenly across the face. Turn off the flash.
4. **Shoot from above.** Hold your phone directly over your baby's face, level with the body, so the head is centred and straight.
5. **Take lots of shots.** Use burst mode and take 20 or more pictures. A calm moment can last only a second.
6. **Check each photo.** Look for shadows from your own head or arms, folds in the sheet, and any part of a hand, toy or dummy in the frame.

## Rules for Toddlers and Young Children

Children under 6 get extra leeway, but you still want the clearest photo you can get.

- **Looking at the camera.** Children under 6 do not have to look directly at the camera, but a forward-facing head makes it easier to meet the size and position rules.
- **Expression.** Children under 6 do not need a plain expression. A calm, natural look is fine, but a wide open mouth or a scrunched face can still make the photo unusable.
- **Eyes.** Eyes should be open and visible where possible, with no hair falling across them.
- **No toys or hands.** Nothing should block the face, and your child must be alone in the photo.

If your child will not sit still, take lots of pictures in one go. Distracting them with a quiet game just before you shoot often works better than telling them to smile or stay still. A helper holding a toy just above or beside the phone can also bring their eyes to the lens.

## Rules for Older Children and Teenagers

From age 6, the standard adult rules apply:

- Neutral expression with the mouth closed
- Looking straight at the camera with eyes open
- No hats or head coverings, unless worn for religious or medical reasons, provided the face is fully visible from chin to forehead
- Hair away from the eyes and face
- No filters, editing effects or heavy retouching

Glasses are generally best removed, because glare and reflections are a common reason photos are rejected. If your child needs to wear glasses for medical reasons, check the official GOV.UK guidance before you apply.

## Digital or Printed: Which Photo Does Your Child Need?

How you apply decides what kind of photo you need.

- **Online application:** You need a digital photo. You can get one from a photo booth or shop that gives you a code, or take one on your own device during the application. GOV.UK crops the image for you, so do not crop it yourself.
- **Paper application:** You need 2 identical printed photos, 35 mm wide by 45 mm tall, on plain white photographic paper with no border. They must not be cut down from a larger picture, and they must not have creases, marks or tears.

GOV.UK says photos from booths and shops are more likely to be approved than photos taken on your own device. If you do take your own, follow the rules closely and check the result before you submit.

## Tips for Taking a Great Child Passport Photo at Home

Small details make a big difference. These tips help you avoid the most common rejections:

- **Use natural light.** A spot near a window gives soft, even lighting. Avoid direct sunlight, which causes squinting and harsh shadows.
- **Choose a plain background.** A white or cream wall, door or sheet works well. Avoid patterned wallpaper, furniture or curtains.
- **Get to eye level.** Hold the camera at your child's face height so the photo is not taken from above or below (except the lying-down newborn trick).
- **Keep some distance.** Stand about one to two metres away and zoom in slightly, rather than holding the camera very close. This avoids distortion.
- **Remove distractions.** Take off hats, hair clips or bows that cast shadows. Keep dummies and toys out of the frame.
- **Dress for contrast.** A top that is clearly different from the background, such as a darker colour against a light wall, helps the head stand out.
- **Time it well.** Shoot after a feed and a nap, not before. Tired and hungry children are the hardest to photograph.
- **Take lots of shots.** Use burst mode and expect to take 20 or more pictures to get one good one.
- **Check for shadows and red-eye.** Look closely at the finished photo, especially around the chin, nose and behind the head.
- **Be patient.** Take a few minutes, or a few attempts on different days. A calm child gives a much better result.

## Common Reasons Child Passport Photos Are Rejected

Knowing what goes wrong can save you weeks of waiting. Photos are often rejected because of:

- Shadows across the face or on the background
- A patterned, dark or cluttered background
- Blurry, grainy or low-resolution images
- Another person, or their hand, visible in the shot
- A dummy, toy or blanket covering part of the face
- Red-eye or glare from a flash
- Photos that have been edited or use filters
- Head too small, too large or cut off in the frame
- A photo taken more than a month before you apply
- A digital photo cropped by hand instead of left for GOV.UK to crop

Before you submit, look at your photo on a large screen and ask: is the face clear, well lit and centred, with a plain background behind it? If the answer is yes, you are usually in good shape.

## Do Children Need a Passport Photo Every Time?

Yes. A child's face changes quickly, so a new photo is needed each time you apply for or renew a passport, even if your child looks the same. Child passports are also valid for a shorter period than adult ones, so you may need a fresh photo more often. Take the photo close to the time you apply, because it must have been taken in the last month and should be easy to recognise at the border.

Run through this quick list before you submit your child's photo:
