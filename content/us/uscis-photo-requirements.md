## What are the USCIS photo requirements?

A USCIS photo is a **{{spec.uscis-photo.size}}** color print on a **white to off-white** background. Your head must measure **{{spec.uscis-photo.headIn}}** from the top of your hair to the bottom of your chin.

Every USCIS form that asks for photos uses this same wording, so one photo file works for all of them. Print it on thin, glossy paper, and don’t mount or edit it.

## Which USCIS forms need photos?

Most forms that lead to a card or travel document ask for **2 identical photos**, and a few ask for none. The table follows the current instructions for each form. Click a form to open its page on uscis.gov.

{{block:uscisFormTable}}

If you file several forms together, prepare a separate set of photos for each form that asks for them. A package with Forms I-485, I-765 and I-131 can need 6 prints for one person.

## Who sets the photo rules: USCIS or the State Department?

Two agencies write U.S. photo rules, and they don’t share one rulebook.

| Document | Who sets the photo rules | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Green card, EAD, travel document, naturalization | USCIS | The instructions for your form on uscis.gov |
| U.S. passport | State Department | travel.state.gov |
| U.S. visa and DV lottery entry | State Department | travel.state.gov and the DV instructions |

The size is 2 × 2 in for all of them. Other details, such as glasses, photo age and digital file rules, come from the agency that handles your document. Don’t assume a rule from one carries over to the other.

## When does USCIS take your photo instead?

USCIS takes your photo itself at a **biometrics appointment** at an Application Support Center. Since **December 12, 2025**, those are the only photos it uses on the cards it issues.

- **Always a new photo:** Forms I-485, I-90, N-400 and N-600.
- **Reuse possible:** For other forms, USCIS may reuse a biometrics photo taken **no more than 3 years** before you file.
- **No self-submitted photos on cards:** USCIS will not use or reuse the prints you mail.

This change did not remove the photo instructions on Forms I-485, I-765 or I-131. Send the photos your form asks for. The detailed guides for [green card](/us/green-card-photo-requirements) and [EAD](/us/ead-i-765-photo-requirements) photos explain what this means for each case.

## How do you label and send USCIS photos?

On the back of every print, write your **name and A-Number**. Leave the A-Number off if you don’t have one yet, and press lightly with a pencil or felt pen.

1. Print every set from **one photo file**, so the copies match.
2. Make sure no print sits on **card backing**. USCIS asks for unmounted photos.
3. Keep each form’s photos with that form in your package.
4. For Forms I-130 (spouse) and I-129F, check that the photos are **under 30 days old** on the day you file.

## Common USCIS photo problems

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Head too small or too large | Crop so your head is {{spec.uscis-photo.headIn}} from hair to chin. |
| Off-color or patterned background | Retake the photo with a bare white or off-white wall behind you. |
| Edited or filtered photo | Use the original camera file. USCIS asks for unretouched photos. |
| Too few prints for a combined package | Count photos form by form, using the table above. |
| Old photos on an I-130 or I-129F | Take new photos within 30 days of filing. |
| Name written in ballpoint that shows through | Write lightly in pencil or felt pen. |
