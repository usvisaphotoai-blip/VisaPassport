## What are the green card photo requirements?

USCIS wants **two identical color photos** that are **{{spec.uscis-photo.size}}**, taken recently. Your head must fill **{{spec.uscis-photo.headIn}}** of the frame, from the top of your hair to the bottom of your chin.

Don’t mount or retouch the photos. USCIS defines unretouched as “not edited or digitally enhanced.” These rules come from the Form I-485 instructions, and the other USCIS forms use the same wording.

## How many photos do you need for each green card form?

You need **2 identical photos for Form I-485**, but other green card forms differ. Some ask for none, and some ask for photos only in special cases. The table below follows each form’s current instructions.

{{block:uscisFormTable}}

A typical family-based package often combines Form I-485 with Forms I-765 and I-131. Each form that asks for photos needs its own set. So a package with all three can need **6 photos for one applicant**. Put each set with its own form, and follow the instructions for every form you file.

## What changed for green card photos in December 2025?

On **December 12, 2025**, USCIS changed how it gets the photo for green cards and other cards it issues. Policy Alert PA-2025-29 says three things:

1. USCIS **will not use or reuse self-submitted photos** on the documents it issues.
2. It can reuse a photo from an earlier biometrics appointment only if that photo is **no more than 3 years old** when you file.
3. Forms I-485, I-90, N-400 and N-600 **always need a new photo**, taken at a biometrics appointment.

The I-485 instructions did not drop the photo request. So mail your two prints as the instructions say, and expect a biometrics appointment notice after you file. Several commercial sites now say USCIS no longer accepts mailed photos at all. The alert itself does not say that.

## How is a green card photo different from a DV lottery photo?

A green card application photo is a **printed 2 × 2 in photo** that you mail with your forms. A DV lottery photo is a **digital file** that you upload to the State Department’s entry form.

| | Green card application (USCIS) | DV lottery entry (State Department) |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Printed photos, mailed | Digital image, uploaded |
| Number | 2 identical (Form I-485) | Set by that year’s DV instructions |
| Who sets the rules | USCIS form instructions | The DV program instructions for each year |
| Where it ends up | Your USCIS file; the card uses your biometrics photo | Your DV entry |

The DV program instructions set their own digital photo rules each year. Use our [DV lottery photo checker](/diversity-visa-lottery-photo-checker) for those, and don’t reuse a DV file for your USCIS prints without resizing it.

## How do you make a green card photo at home?

You can take a compliant photo with any phone. Then crop it and print it at a photo lab.

1. **Use a plain wall in white or off-white as your backdrop.** Face a window so daylight lights your face without shadows.
2. **Ask someone else to take the photo** from about arm’s length or farther, with the camera at your eye level.
3. **Face the camera straight on** with both eyes open and a neutral expression. Keep your head bare unless you wear religious headwear.
4. **Upload the photo to the tool above.** It crops the image to {{spec.uscis-photo.sizeIn}} and checks that your head is {{spec.uscis-photo.headIn}} tall.
5. **Print on glossy photo paper** at a photo lab. A home printer often gives thick or matte prints, which USCIS does not ask for.
6. **Label the back lightly** with your name and A-Number in pencil or felt pen. Press softly so the writing doesn’t show through.

**A note on editing:** USCIS rejects edited or digitally enhanced photos. A plain light wall means the tool only has to crop and resize your photo. Skip beauty filters, skin smoothing and face-shape effects completely.

## Why does USCIS reject green card photos?

Most rejected photos break one of a few rules. Here is how to fix each one.

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Head too small or too large | Crop so your head is {{spec.uscis-photo.headIn}} from hair to chin. |
| Gray, blue or patterned background | Retake the photo with a bare white or off-white wall behind you. |
| Shadows behind your head or on your face | Face a window and step about 2 feet away from the wall. |
| Filter, smoothing or other edits | Use the original camera file. USCIS asks for unretouched photos. |
| Photo mounted on card or backing | Send plain, unmounted prints, and follow your form’s instructions for where to put them. |
| Two prints that don’t match | Print both from the same file at the same time. |
| Writing pressed through the front | Write lightly in pencil or felt pen, never with a ballpoint. |
| Photo too old for an I-130 or I-129F | Take new photos within 30 days before you file. |

## What about children, glasses and head coverings?

**Children and babies:** Every applicant needs their own photos, whatever their age. The I-485 instructions set no special rules for children. Lay a baby on a plain white sheet and photograph them from directly above. Keep hands and toys out of the frame.

**Glasses:** The USCIS instructions don’t mention glasses. Take them off if you can, because lens glare can hide your eyes.

**Head coverings:** Your head must be bare, unless you wear headwear required by your religion. Keep your full face visible from your hairline to your chin.
