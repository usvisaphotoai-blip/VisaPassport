## Outdated or contradictory items

- **PPO contradicts itself on glasses**: one paragraph says prescription glasses can stay on if there is no glare, another says they are banned with a doctor's-note exception, and a third says that rule changed "Until 2016".
- **PPO's digital specs (600–1200 px, JPEG only, ≤240 kB, 20:1)** match what PG lists for visa/DV photos, not what PG and IMR list for online renewal. PPO never names the channel those numbers apply to.
- **PPO camera distance**: "about 20 inches" and "about 3 feet" on the same page.
- **PPO prices are inconsistent**: $19.99 and $16.95 for what reads like the same service.
- **PA head height "22 mm to 35 mm"** vs everyone else's "25–35 mm" (PA's own inch values match the others).
- **PA widget head height 1.29 in vs PPO widget 1.23 in** (same company family, same layout, different number).
- **PA is a USCIS page**: it says selfies "are not allowed" and also says smiling slightly is allowed while listing smiling as a mistake.
- **VF "600 pixels per inch (ppi)"** vs IMR "300 DPI" vs PPO/PA "301 dpi".
- **VF says glasses have "medical or religious" exceptions.** Nobody else mentions a religious exception for glasses.
- **VF dated 2023-05-30** in structured data. Its digital answer only says "a new online renewal option exists".
- **XPP "photo does not have to be square" for digital submissions** vs PG/PPO, which say the digital photo is square. XPP also says "Renewals by mail can use digital submission in some cases", which contradicts every other page (mail = print).
- **XPP baby tip "Make sure the baby's eyes are open"** vs VF/PG "it is okay if their eyes are not entirely open" for infants.
- **PG, glasses**: "Sunglasses and tinted glasses aren't allowed either, even for medical reasons" sits next to a medical-statement exception. The page also both allows a "natural closed-mouth smile" and lists "smiles … shows teeth" as a violation (consistent, but the wording is loose).
- **PG paper**: "matte or glossy" in the checklist but "Use glossy photo paper" in the print FAQ.

## Conflicts between competitors

1. **Online/digital pixel dimensions**: 600×600–1200×1200 (PPO) / 600×600 minimum, no maximum (IMR) / at least 600×600, need not be square (XPP) / "does not set a pixel size" (PG).
2. **Digital file size**: ≤240 kB (PPO) / under 6 MB (IMR) / 54 KB–10 MB (PG).
3. **Digital file format**: JPEG only (PPO, IMR) / JPG, JPEG, PNG, HEIC, HEIF (PG).
4. **Glasses exception**: none at all (IMR) / medical only (XPP, PA, PG) / medical or religious (VF) / prescription OK without glare (PPO, one paragraph).
5. **Smiling**: natural smile OK (PPO, PG), slight closed-lip smile OK (VF), "you'd better not" (XPP), smiling slightly OK but also listed as a mistake (PA).
6. **Head height in mm**: 25–35 (most) vs 22–35 (PA).
7. **Print resolution**: 300 DPI (IMR) vs 301 dpi (PPO, PA) vs 600 ppi minimum (VF).
8. **Selfies**: not allowed (PA, PPO) vs allowed if compliant (VF, PG).
9. **Camera distance**: 20 in / 3 ft (PPO), 16–20 in front / 4–6 ft rear (PA), 5–7 ft (PG).
10. **Background wording**: white or off-white (5 pages) vs "plain light background" (PA).
11. **Child concessions** (from pages that mention them): VF "Children under 6 years are not required to look directly at the camera or maintain a neutral expression" vs PG "Ages 1 to 5 … must look directly into the camera with a neutral expression".
12. **DS-160 equals passport**: IMR says "Dimensionally, yes — completely" (600×600, same spec), while PG and PPO give DS-160/visa-style limits (≤240 KB, 1200 px max) that differ from online renewal.

## Gaps: what none of them cover well

1. **No dated official citation.** Only IMR, XPP and PG link travel.state.gov, and none gives an access date or quotes the official wording next to the number. PPO, VF and PA link no official source at all. Nobody notes that travel.state.gov URLs appear to differ between pages (`/content/travel/en/passports/how-apply/photos.html` in IMR/XPP/PG vs `/en/passports/apply/help/photos.html` in visafoto's baby page and in search results), so a reader can't tell which page is current.
2. **No single mm / in / px table that covers head and eye position.** IMR gives in + mm for head and eye. Nobody converts the head range or eye line into pixels for a 600×600 (or any) digital file, even though several push 600 px files.
3. **Print rules and digital rules are mixed together.** Only PG separates DS-11, DS-82 mail and online renewal in a table, and even PG's pixel claim differs from its own digital guide. PPO blends visa-style digital limits into a passport page without saying so. XPP says mail renewals can be digital.
4. **No rejection-fix table** (symptom → cause → fix). PG and PA have bullet lists of mistakes, IMR has a one-paragraph FAQ, and nobody maps each rejection reason to a concrete fix or retake step.
5. **No "how to measure your own photo" walkthrough.** Nobody explains with a diagram how to check head height or eye line on a print with a ruler, or on a file in pixels.
6. **The conflicts themselves go unaddressed.** No page acknowledges that sources disagree (pixel limits, file size, glasses exceptions, smiling) or says how a reader should resolve that (go to the official page and check the date).
7. **Little on special cases.** Hearing aids and medical devices get one line (PG). Nothing covers disability accommodations beyond a PG link, and nothing covers tattoos, facial piercings (except a passing mention), heavy beards, or photos of people who can't stand.
8. **Black-and-white and scanned photos**: only VF answers these, and nobody explains photocopy vs scan vs print-from-file.
9. **Printing at home**: nobody gives printer settings (actual size, no scaling) except IMR, and nobody covers checking the cut size after printing.
10. **Thin structured comparisons**: no page has a clear "allowed / not allowed" two-column table for clothing, jewelry, hair, makeup, head coverings and glasses with a source per row.
11. **Off-topic/weak pages rank**: PA is a USCIS page and XPP is a 750-word hub whose specs sit in an image. That is room for a single complete, text-based, sourced page.
