## Unique elements

- **PhotoGov:** an 11-row spec table; a side-by-side table comparing the E-DV entry photo with the interview photo; a table of photo services (time, budget and approval chance for each); a 4-scenario "what to do if rejected" walkthrough; a 5-category rejection breakdown with percentages; an 8-step home-shoot guide; a baby section citing 9 FAM 504.4-4(C); a list of other US photo formats with file limits; and links to official sources (travel.state.gov, dvprogram.state.gov, 9 FAM, 22 CFR §42.33, ICAO 9303). It also has acceptable and unacceptable example images.
- **Passlens:** a compact 3-column table (Requirement | Official direction | Why it matters) and links to 4 official State Department pages, including the "Changes to the 2027 DV entry period" notice. It is the only competitor that frames the page as "entry-level compliance" across all family members.
- **PhotoAiD:** a spec box (2x2 in, 301 dpi, head 1.29 in, eye line 1.18 in); a downloadable "Google Sheets checklist"; an upload widget; and a human expert check claim ("feedback in under 60 seconds"). Its only official link is to dvprogram.state.gov.

## Conflicts between competitors (and internal contradictions)

1. **Pixel size.** All three say 600 × 600 for DV. PhotoGov insists on "exactly", saying both minimum and maximum are 600 and that a "600 × 599 frame fails". Passlens's own DS-160 guide says 600–1200 px, and visafoto.com (seen in search) says "any square between 600 and 1200 pixels". So it is disputed whether DV accepts sizes above 600 px.
2. **Head-size tolerance.** In search snippets, visafoto and one other site give head height as 50–70%; the three competitors say 50–69%. PhotoAiD gives the print head size as "1 inch and 1 3/8 inches (22 and 35 mm)", but 1 inch is 25.4 mm, not 22 mm. PhotoGov gives "25-35 mm".
3. **Print size on a digital-only entry.** PhotoAiD frames the DV entry photo as "2x2 in / 301 dpi / printable" and its FAQ says the "correct DV lottery photo size is 2x2 inches". PhotoGov and Passlens say no print is used at the entry stage. Also, 600 px over 2 in is 300 dpi, not 301.
4. **Can you fix a rejected upload in the same window?** PhotoGov contradicts itself. It says entries "are dropped during the registration window — with no notification and no opportunity to resubmit" and "the second attempt isn't available inside the same annual window". Its FAQ then says "you still have time to correct and resubmit before the registration window closes".
5. **Is there an automated check at upload?** PhotoGov says "Every parameter listed below is enforced by the upload form" and lists head/eye position and "no visible signs of AI generation". It also says the form "returns only a generic error" and that rejections are silent. Passlens and PhotoAiD make no claims about what the upload form checks.
6. **Dates.** PhotoAiD's FAQ is a cycle out of date (DV-2026 dates from Oct–Nov 2024; status check "Starting May 3, 2025"). PhotoGov (May 2026) says DV-2027 dates were not yet announced. Neither covers what happened after May 2026, and both pages are stale as of 2026-11-12.
7. **Eyewear exceptions.** PhotoGov says the eyeglass exemption was "withdrawn in November 2016 and has not been restored". PhotoAiD says only "no eyeglasses". Neither mentions the medical-exception statement process that the State Department describes for passports. Check whether it applies to DV.
8. **Unsourced claims in PhotoGov.** The rejection percentages ("Department of State data"), the "similarity engine" and "AI-detection systems", and the claim that the State Department's "DV Photo Validator ... reports those three checks" are all stated without a citation. Treat them as unverified.

## Elements they all cover

- JPEG, 240 KB maximum, square 600 × 600
- Taken within the last 6 months; no reuse of a prior-year DV photo
- Plain white or off-white background
- Neutral expression with both eyes open
- No eyeglasses
- Religious head coverings allowed only if the face stays visible (PhotoGov and PhotoAiD; Passlens mentions only "daily religious clothing")
- No digital manipulation that changes your appearance
- A separate photo for every family member on the entry
- A link to dvprogram.state.gov

## Gaps: what none of them cover well

1. **Current DV-2027/DV-2028 status as of Nov 2026.** No competitor says whether the DV-2027 registration actually ran or was changed, or what the DV-2028 timeline is. PhotoGov stops at "not yet announced (May 2026)" and PhotoAiD shows DV-2026 dates. A dated "what's current" box, checked against travel.state.gov, would be the biggest win.
2. **How to make a 600 × 600 file under 240 KB on a phone, step by step.** PhotoGov gives JPEG quality percentages but no concrete workflow for iPhone (HEIC to JPEG, Settings > Camera > Formats > Most Compatible), Android, Windows Photos, or macOS Preview (Tools > Adjust Size, Export quality slider). None show how to read a file's pixel size and KB on each OS.
3. **Real checks vs folklore.** No one cleanly separates what the official instructions state from what vendors infer. Examples of vendor inference: exact head/eye bands enforced at upload, "similarity engine" claims, rejection percentages.
4. **What the confirmation page and Entrant Status Check show,** and how a bad photo actually surfaces (at upload, at selection, or at the interview). This is only partly covered by PhotoGov's 4 scenarios, which contradict themselves.
5. **Scanning an existing print correctly.** Only PhotoAiD gives 300 ppi on a 2x2 print. No one explains that the scan must then be cropped to 600 × 600 and recompressed, or warns that scanning a passport or ID photo isn't allowed (Passlens alone mentions this).
6. **Family-entry logistics.** None cover how to name and track a file per person, the case of a spouse or child who can't be photographed (deceased? whereabouts unknown?), or what happens if a child turns 21.
7. **Scam and fee warnings.** The entry is free and no third party is needed. Only PhotoAiD's FAQ touches on this ("Don't let others hold [your confirmation number]"). No one links the State Department's fraud warning or explains that a paid "DV photo service" cannot improve your odds.
8. **Interview-stage photo.** PhotoGov's comparison table covers it, but no one says clearly that the selectee's DS-260 upload and the interview prints are separate photos that must be taken fresh.
9. **Accessibility cases.** None cover how to photograph an applicant who uses a wheelchair, who can't hold their head upright, or who needs glasses for orientation.
