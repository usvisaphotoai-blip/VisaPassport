## Unique elements

- **PhotoAiD:** short retailer-by-retailer H3s, an FAQ covering UPS, Sam's Club, AAA and Rite Aid, and a photobooth price range. It links to no official or retailer sources.
- **PhotoGov:** a comparison table (provider | price | what you get); a table comparing online tools (including competitors' prices); a State Department fee table split by book, card and both; a "passport photo for under $1" walkthrough (4x6 print at $0.42); a "why counters cost more" explanation; a section on avoiding a second charge; a baby/child cost section; and an "Official Sources" list linking retailer pages and travel.state.gov. It is the only one that says when prices were checked ("checked August 2026" for FedEx).
- **Visafoto:** 4x6 print prices at Walgreens, CVS, FedEx, Rite Aid and Walmart; a "do you have to pay?" framing; and a 7ID app plug.

## Conflicts between competitors (and internal contradictions)

1. **CVS:** $16.99 (PhotoAiD, Visafoto) vs $17.99 (PhotoGov). On digital copies, PhotoAiD says "only possible to get physical copies" but its own FAQ says "add a digital copy for $3.99". PhotoAiD also gives two different prices for extra copies ("$2.99 ... total of $19.98" vs "$3.99").
2. **Walgreens digital copy:** PhotoAiD says "does not currently provide digital passport pictures". PhotoGov says it includes "a free digital copy delivered by email".
3. **Walmart:** $7.44 (PhotoAiD) vs $7.64 (PhotoGov). PhotoGov says "upload your photo online for same-day pickup", but Walmart's own page (checked below) says "Passport photos are currently a Photo Center in-store service only."
4. **FedEx:** $14.95 (PhotoAiD, Visafoto) vs $15.95 (PhotoGov).
5. **UPS Store:** a flat $11.99 (PhotoAiD, Visafoto) vs "$12 to $35, varies by store" (PhotoGov).
6. **Rite Aid:** $8.99 (PhotoAiD) vs $14.99 (Visafoto). None of them say whether Rite Aid stores still operate, and riteaid.com blocked access on 2026-11-12 (see below), so availability is unverified. **This needs an official check before Rite Aid appears on our page at all.**
7. **AAA:** PhotoAiD gives a flat "members $10, non members $15", but AAA pricing is set by each regional club (one club, checked below, lists four tiers).
8. **Studio range:** $10–20 (PhotoAiD) vs $20–50 / $20–100 (PhotoGov) vs $25–50+ (Visafoto).
9. **Overall range:** "$7–17" (PhotoAiD) vs "$0 to $17.99" (PhotoGov). PhotoAiD's range also excludes its own $8.99 Rite Aid and $5.31 Sam's Club figures.
10. **1–3 day return delivery:** PhotoGov says "$23.36". USPS's own page (checked below) says "$22.05".
11. **PhotoGov's dates:** the page is "Published/Last update: August 19, 2026" yet says prices are "current for September 2026".
12. **Title vs H1 years:** PhotoAiD's title says 2025 and its H1 says 2026. Visafoto's URL says 2024 and its title says 2026.
13. **Currency:** Visafoto quotes its own price as "₹600" on a US page.

## Elements they all cover

- Price per retailer for CVS, Walgreens, USPS, FedEx and UPS (Walmart in two of the three)
- Photo studio price range
- The DIY or online-tool option as the cheapest path, which each one uses to promote its own product
- Printing a 4x6 template (PhotoGov and Visafoto give per-print prices)

## Gaps: what none of them cover well

1. **Dated, sourced prices for each retailer.** Only PhotoGov dates one price (FedEx, "checked August 2026"). Nobody shows a "checked on" date per row with the retailer URL, and two of the three pages show prices that are out of date or contradict each other.
2. **What you get for the price.** Number of prints, whether a digital copy is included, the USB option, and whether a retake is free if the photo is rejected. This is only partial in PhotoGov and inconsistent elsewhere.
3. **Availability caveats.** "Select locations only" (CVS, USPS by appointment), Walmart in-store only, the Costco status, and the Rite Aid status. No one has a clear "does X still do passport photos?" check with a date.
4. **Membership pricing.** AAA tiers by club, Sam's Club and Costco membership requirements, and student or military discounts are not covered or covered only as a single flat number.
5. **Total cost of getting a passport.** Only PhotoGov combines photo cost with fees, and its delivery-fee figure conflicts with USPS. Nobody gives worked totals (for example, first-time adult book via USPS: photo + application fee + execution fee ± expedite ± 1–3 day delivery).
6. **Online renewal.** Nobody explains that online renewal needs only a digital file (no print), which changes the cheapest option, or what file the retailer gives you and whether it meets the online renewal upload spec.
7. **Family math.** The cost for a family of four, with retake risk for babies. PhotoGov only says a child's photo costs "the same".
8. **Getting a photo free.** For example, AAA Premier/Plus free sets, and some acceptance facilities or libraries. Not covered except AAA in passing.
9. **Hidden costs:** printing your own 2x2 at home (paper, ink), cutting accuracy, and the risk of a mail-in rejection delaying an application (PhotoGov touches on this in "Avoiding a Second Charge").
