## Unique elements

- **PhotoAiD:** acceptable and unacceptable smile example images; links to the CBS News "no teeth" story, BBC (UK 2004) and travel.state.gov; the in-house 10,000-photo stat; "smiled in a passport photo, what now?" section.
- **PhotoGov:** three tables (official rule spec; "Officially Acceptable vs Best Practice"; common expression errors with reasons); tips for people who smile automatically (exhale, burst mode); a 5-point pre-submission checklist; the visa-vs-passport distinction; an "Official Government Sources" list (travel.state.gov passport photos, online renewal photo page, US visa photo page, 22 CFR §51.27, ICAO 9303).
- **Passport-Photo.Online:** example images of acceptable and unacceptable smiles for adults and for children; a link to a "brief history of passport photos" article.

## Conflicts between competitors

1. **Recommendation.** PhotoGov says "Don't smile". PhotoAiD says "the choice is yours". Passport-Photo.Online says a gentle smile is fine.
2. **Children.** PhotoGov: "A neutral facial expression is required for children". PhotoAiD: "kids can smile"; "Newborns and toddlers can have their mouths open". Passport-Photo.Online: "Children are allowed to have a broader smile". These directly contradict each other. PhotoGov also contradicts itself ("isn't recommended" vs "required").
3. **Does a slight smile cause automatic rejection?** PhotoGov says a slight smile "can trigger an automatic biometric verification failure" but also says it's officially allowed. The other two say a closed-mouth smile is acceptable. None cites any evidence of automated rejection for a closed-mouth smile.
4. **The 2004 date.** PhotoAiD dates it to "around 2004" from forums and newspapers, and its sources are a CBS story and a BBC UK story. Passport-Photo.Online states "Since 2004" as fact. The BBC link is about UK rules, not US.
5. **Other countries.** PhotoAiD says "In most European countries, like the UK, grins are entirely banned". Search snippets from other sites say smiling is allowed in Germany and the UK "only if the facial expression will not impact the clarity of the face". These framings differ.
6. **H1 vs title.** Passport-Photo.Online's H1 targets "show teeth" while its title targets "smile". PhotoAiD's title says "Yes…And No" while its body says "Yes".

## Elements they all cover

- A closed-mouth smile is allowed for US passports; no teeth; eyes open
- The reason: biometric and facial recognition
- A neutral expression is safest
- Children get some leeway (they differ on how much)
- Example images or the official photo examples

## Gaps: what none of them cover well

1. **Exact official wording, dated and quoted.** Only PhotoGov quotes a single State Department line. No one quotes 22 CFR §51.27 text (PhotoGov only links it) or the State Department's photo-requirements and photo-examples pages verbatim with a "checked on" date.
2. **Where the smile rule differs by document.** PhotoGov covers only visa vs passport, and only in one line. Nobody covers the passport card (same photo), online renewal upload, the DS-160 visa, green card / USCIS, the DV lottery entry, or the REAL ID / state DMV, each of which has its own wording.
3. **Who decides "exaggerated" and when.** No one explains the actual review path (acceptance agent vs passport agency adjudicator vs online-renewal photo review) or what a photo-rejection letter or email looks like and how long a resubmission takes.
4. **Facial differences and medical conditions.** No one covers people whose resting face looks like a smile, facial paralysis or Bell's palsy, braces or dental appliances, or a mouth that can't fully close. These are real long-tail queries.
5. **Retake logistics by channel.** For example, getting a free retake at retailers if the photo is rejected for expression, or what to do if your online renewal is held for a photo issue.
6. **Infants: what "neutral" means in practice.** All three are vague. None give clear age bands (under 1, toddler, school-age) with what's acceptable for each, and they contradict each other.
7. **A quick self-check method,** such as comparing mouth corners to a relaxed baseline or checking that the cheeks aren't raised. PhotoGov gives a burst-mode tip but no visual criteria.
8. **Evidence.** No competitor cites any data on smile-related rejection rates beyond PhotoAiD's in-house stat.
