## Unique elements

- **PG**: age-band table (newborn / 2–6 mo / 6–12 mo / toddler / preschool / 5+) with eyes, expression, support and capture method per band; 8-step home routine; 12 rejection causes; a "4 scenarios" rejected-photo playbook (mail notice / at appointment / paid service / at the border); 20-question FAQ including **twins**, car seat, pacifier, iPhone transfer warning (WhatsApp/iMessage recompression), and editing limits; cites 22 CFR, 9 FAM and ICAO; links DS-11 PDF and the "Children Under 16" page.
- **VF**: concise 10-bullet spec list; the only one with a dated "Last updated" (September 29, 2026); says clearly that under-16 = printed photo, not digital; refund policy; links to the State Department photo page at `travel.state.gov/en/passports/apply/help/photos.html` (a different URL path from the other two).
- **XPP**: embeds State Department acceptable/unacceptable child example images ("Image source: U.S. Department of State"); two capture options (lying flat / car seat); very short.

## Outdated or contradictory items

- **PG contradicts itself on closed eyes**: "Fully closed eyes are not accepted at any age" vs the FAQ "Can a Newborn Baby Have Their Eyes Closed …? Yes." and the rejection list "Closed eyes. Accepted as a partial concession for infants under twelve months".
- **PG contradicts itself on when the adult standard starts**: "From the first birthday onward" vs "From about five years old". Photogov's general requirements page uses "Ages 6 to 15" as the adult-standard band.
- **PG photo count** ("two identical … photographs" with DS-11) conflicts with photogov's own US requirements page ("One passport picture is required per submission") and with VF ("one photo").
- **PG gives digital specs for babies** (600–1200 px, JPEG, ≤240 KB) for "digital channels such as the DV Lottery, DS-160, or DS-260". These aren't passport rules, and the page's H1 calls itself "Digital Passport Photos", which blurs this.
- **PG's "Wrong file format. HEIC straight from an iPhone"** conflicts with photogov's digital guide, where HEIC is accepted for online renewal (a different channel, but the pages don't reconcile it).
- **PG's step 8** says crop to "often 2 × 2 inches or 35 × 45 mm". That is generic, non-US text on a US page.
- **PG allows "white-balance correction"**, while photogov's US requirements page bans "color correction".
- **XPP's own US hub page** (xpassportphoto.com/blog/us-passport-photo) says "Make sure the baby's eyes are open", which contradicts this baby page ("acceptable if their eyes are not fully open").
- **VF's general FAQ page** (visafoto.com/usa/passport-photo-faq) says "Children under 6 years are not required to look directly at the camera or maintain a neutral expression" and "infants under 1-year-old … eyes … closed" is acceptable. Its baby page is vaguer ("A baby's eyes do not have to be entirely open") and gives no age cut-off.

## Conflicts between competitors

1. **Number of prints with DS-11**: two identical (PG) vs one (VF).
2. **Closed eyes**: never fully closed (PG, one line) vs "not fully open" acceptable under 1 (XPP) vs "do not have to be entirely open" with no age limit (VF) vs closed acceptable under 1 (VF FAQ page).
3. **Age at which the concessions end**: 12 months (PG, XPP) vs 5 years (PG, another line) vs 6 years (VF FAQ page, photogov general page).
4. **Looking at the camera**: newborns exempt (PG) vs "directly facing the camera" (XPP).
5. **Digital relevance**: PG presents digital specs for children vs VF "printed photo, not a digital upload".
6. **Editing**: white-balance/brightness OK (PG); the others don't address it, and photogov's general page bans colour correction.
7. **Price of in-store baby photos**: "$12–18" / "$15–18" (PG) vs "$15" for 2 (VF).

## Elements they all cover

- 2 x 2 in / 51 x 51 mm, white or off-white background
- No parent hands or supports in the frame
- No pacifiers or toys
- Some eyes-not-fully-open concession for infants
- Home capture on a white sheet, baby lying down and shot from above
- A plug for the site's own tool

## Gaps: what none of them cover well

1. **Premature, NICU and medical-device babies.** Nothing on oxygen cannulas, feeding tubes, monitors, helmets (plagiocephaly), casts or hospital wristbands. No mention of how a photo taken in hospital or on a hospital blanket fares.
2. **Twins and multiples**: only PG, in one FAQ. Nobody covers labelling, the risk of mixing up prints, or the logistics of one session for two babies.
3. **Measuring head size on a lying-down baby.** Nobody explains crown-to-chin on a baby whose chin disappears into rolls or whose head is turned, or how cropping from an overhead shot works in practice (no diagram, no px guidance).
4. **No rejection-fix table** (symptom → fix). PG has a list plus 4 generic scenarios, and nobody maps "hand visible / shadow under chin / eyes closed (over 1) / blanket texture" to a specific retake fix.
5. **Conflicting age thresholds go unaddressed.** None reconciles under-1 vs under-6 concessions, and none quotes the official wording with a date.
6. **No dated official citation.** PG lists sources without dates, VF has one link, and XPP credits images only. Nobody quotes the State Department's actual child-photo sentence.
7. **Print vs digital for children isn't explained clearly.** Only VF says under-16 = print. No page explains that a child can't renew online, or how that changes the photo you need (PG covers renewal only for validity).
8. **Newborn timing.** Nothing on the 6-month rule for a newborn whose face changes fast, or what to do if the application is delayed after the photo is taken.
9. **Photo of a baby with a religious or medical head covering, or a hat for a newborn's warmth.** Not covered beyond "unless religious".
10. **Skin-tone and lighting problems specific to infants** (jaundice, flash red-eye in newborns, white-on-white for very fair babies, dark-skinned babies on white). Only generic lighting advice is given.
11. **Disability or special needs**: PG mentions "children with sensory needs" once. No accommodation guidance.
12. **The physical print.** Nobody covers where the print goes on DS-11 for a child, whether to staple it, or what to bring to the acceptance facility.
